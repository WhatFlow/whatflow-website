## Start with the customer’s job

Calling every COD customer asks the team to spend time on many simple confirmations. A clear WhatsApp request lets the buyer answer asynchronously while staff focus on uncertain addresses, unusual requests and other exceptions.

## Where each approach helps

Calls are still useful when a customer does not respond or needs a complicated explanation. The goal is a sensible confirmation process, not removing human help from every order.

## Reserve the call queue for a defined exception

Build a hypothetical exception queue before assuming every nonresponse needs another call. Distinguish no reply, an explicit decline, an unclear address and a customer asking to speak with someone. Give each case a next step, owner and deadline that fit the dispatch process. Record call attempts and investigation time alongside the cost of the eligible WhatsApp requests. WhatFlow’s shared inbox can make those replies visible to another colleague, but it does not determine whether an order should ship. Review the resulting decision record with the warehouse before rollout. A reduction in routine calls would need observation; this exercise does not predict labor savings or fewer returns.

Test confirmed, declined, late and duplicate replies. Decide how each response should affect the Shopify order and who handles a buyer who never replies before the dispatch cutoff.
