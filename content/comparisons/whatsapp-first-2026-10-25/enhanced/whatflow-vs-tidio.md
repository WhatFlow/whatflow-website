## What Tidio is offering

Tidio's current website presents live chat, a helpdesk, Flows and the Lyro AI agent. It also describes WhatsApp as part of a multichannel inbox. Those capabilities make it relevant to stores rebuilding their customer-service experience, particularly when onsite conversations matter.

Do not assume that a channel in an inbox means every outbound ecommerce journey is available in the same way. Ask for the exact WhatsApp use case, the plan it requires and how it interacts with your Shopify data.

For a concrete baseline, consider 20,000 sent messages in a month. Among the listed WhatFlow plans, Growth produces an app-side cost of $29.00 at that volume. This calculation uses its $29.00 base, 20,000 included sends and $0.0025 rate above the allowance. It assumes a single monthly allowance and does not include tax or Meta delivery charges. The current installation terms and rate pages should be checked before committing a campaign budget.

## Separate an AI answer from an authorized store action

Use a hypothetical customer asking whether a return can be accepted outside the usual conditions. Ask the proposed Tidio experience to distinguish an informative policy answer from an authorized decision that changes the order or promises an exception. Inspect which source informed the answer and how the case reaches the person allowed to decide. Compare that support design with WhatFlow’s order-linked WhatsApp conversation, where the team still owns the business decision. Record the Lyro or helpdesk scope, WhatsApp entitlement and any usage unit in the quote. A convincing AI reply should not be treated as proof that a refund, cancellation or other store operation occurred.

Tidio merits attention when Lyro and website support automation are the main purchase, with WhatsApp as one part of that broader service design.
