## Start with the customer’s job

A warehouse cutoff creates a practical deadline: the team needs to know which COD orders are ready to ship. A direct WhatsApp confirmation can be useful when customers prefer replying there and the message asks for a short, clear decision.

## Where each approach helps

Email remains useful for the complete order receipt and detailed information. WhatsApp can handle the immediate question without forcing the receipt itself to move channels.

## Why I would use WhatFlow for this workflow

Place the short COD decision in the WhatsApp conversation and keep the receipt available by email. Configure the message around the actual order, name the person checking replies and decide how late corrections are handled. A customer response informs the store’s process; it does not automatically authorize every warehouse action.

## Define what a reply before cutoff can authorize

For a hypothetical dispatch day, write the confirmation cutoff beside the customer’s actual decision. A buyer confirming that the order details are correct is not necessarily authorizing every subsequent warehouse operation. Place the full receipt and the short confirmation request on the same timeline, then include a correction arriving after the cutoff. Ask the operator where the current decision appears in Shopify and who reviews the late response. WhatFlow can connect the WhatsApp exchange to the store process while email continues carrying the receipt. Count usable decisions and unresolved exceptions, not opens alone, and keep fulfillment authority in the store’s separately defined rule.

Send controlled test requests with a visible order reference. Include a late reply and a changed order, then check the final Shopify record before any fulfillment action.
