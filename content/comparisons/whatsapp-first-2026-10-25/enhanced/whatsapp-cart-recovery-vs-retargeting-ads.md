## Start with the customer’s job

Retargeting asks a shopper to notice the store again while doing something else. A permissioned WhatsApp reminder can address a specific checkout and let the customer explain what is stopping the purchase.

## Where each approach helps

Ads can reach audiences and buying moments that direct messaging cannot. WhatsApp requires an appropriate contact relationship and should not become repeated pressure. The channels may complement each other if the store coordinates frequency.

## Why I would use WhatFlow for this workflow

I would address the known checkout question in WhatFlow before treating another ad impression as the next useful step. Give replies an owner and keep the purchase exit coordinated. ## Give overlapping attribution one explicit treatment

Create a hypothetical customer timeline containing an ad impression, a site visit, an eligible recovery message and a later order. Ask the marketing owner how each reporting tool would credit that order and how the store will avoid adding those credits together as separate sales. Keep the planned advertising audience distinct from the permissioned messaging audience. Then identify the customer question the WhatsApp route is intended to answer and who will answer it. WhatFlow can provide that direct Shopify conversation without replacing advertising’s acquisition role. Compare campaign costs and returns under a stated method, and use a suitable comparison group before making claims about extra orders caused by either channel.

Choose a controlled pilot and document attribution windows. Review how recent buyers are excluded and whether support replies reveal recurring checkout problems worth fixing on the site.
