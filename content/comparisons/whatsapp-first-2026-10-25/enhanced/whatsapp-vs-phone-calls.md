## Start with the customer’s job

A phone call can resolve a complicated issue quickly, but it also asks the customer and employee to be available at the same moment. WhatsApp lets a shopper confirm an order or clarify a delivery detail when they have time, while leaving a readable trail for the team.

## Where each approach helps

Calls remain useful for urgent or sensitive conversations and people who prefer speaking. WhatsApp is particularly attractive for repeatable questions with a short answer. A good operation can reserve calls for exceptions rather than making every order require one.

## Compare the complete resolution effort for a delayed answer

For a hypothetical follow-up task, include a customer who cannot answer immediately and another whose question needs a detailed conversation. Record attempts, preparation, elapsed waiting and actual handling separately, so asynchronous waiting is not confused with staff work. Decide when an unresolved WhatsApp exchange should become a requested call and who records the resulting decision against the order. WhatFlow’s inbox can keep the written context available to colleagues without seat fees, while a call may resolve an exception more appropriately. Review the total process before predicting labor savings. The useful outcome is an accurate next step that both the buyer and the store understand, regardless of the chosen route.

Use a controlled set of confirmation tasks. Track attempts, elapsed time, response clarity and the final order action. Compare the process cost, not just the price of a connected minute.
