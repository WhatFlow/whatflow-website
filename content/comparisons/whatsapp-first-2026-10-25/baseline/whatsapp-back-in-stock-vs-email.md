## Key takeaways

- An alert is not an inventory reservation.
- Use the specific variant and current destination.
- Email permission does not automatically authorize WhatsApp.

## Start with the customer’s job

A shopper waiting for a product may appreciate a direct message when it returns. WhatsApp is particularly useful if that alert leads to a question about the variant, delivery or how quickly the item may sell out.

## Keep the alert truthful when stock changes again

For a hypothetical restock, select one variant with a short waiting list and record the stock state at audience selection, send preparation and destination opening. Consider a buyer who opens the alert after the last item sells. The message should explain availability without implying a reservation the system does not provide. Keep the email and WhatsApp audiences separate according to the permissions actually recorded, and assign a response for shoppers asking about the next restock. WhatFlow can supply the WhatsApp notification and shared conversation, while the store maintains inventory and destination accuracy. Evaluate useful responses and eligible purchases rather than attributing every later sale to the first alert opened.

Test the specific variant, destination link and notification timing. Include a product that sells out again and decide how the experience should avoid misleading shoppers.
