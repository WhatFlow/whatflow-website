## A worked WhatFlow cost example

For a concrete baseline, consider 10,000 sent messages in a month. Among the listed WhatFlow plans, Growth produces an app-side cost of $29.00 at that volume. This calculation uses its $29.00 base, 20,000 included sends and $0.0025 rate above the allowance. It assumes a single monthly allowance and does not include tax or Meta delivery charges. It is a planning example, not a combined quote for every possible message category or destination.

Keep three lines in the budget: the application, applicable WhatsApp delivery fees, and any separate implementation or services. Meta pricing varies by destination and category, and some service or qualifying utility messages have different treatment. An app allowance does not override Meta’s rules. The current installation terms and rate pages should be checked before committing a campaign budget.

## Check audience cleanup without losing channel preferences

For a hypothetical Mailchimp review, separate a cleaned newsletter audience from the customer records needed for order support. Ask the team to show which email status controls campaigns and how an appropriate WhatsApp preference is stored in the separate messaging process. Do not reactivate marketing permission merely because the customer still needs an order answer. Compare the account’s contact and email-send limits with WhatFlow’s message allowances using their own definitions. Include the colleagues who edit campaigns and those who only help customers, since their access needs differ. The useful result is a maintained email program plus a clearly owned conversation route, not an unnecessary wholesale audience migration.

Mailchimp can remain useful for the broader newsletter and audience program; adding WhatsApp does not require abandoning email.
