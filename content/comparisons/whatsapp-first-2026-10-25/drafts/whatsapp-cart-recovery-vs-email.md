## Start with the customer’s job

A shopper may abandon checkout because they are unsure about delivery, sizing or payment. Another discount email does not necessarily answer that question. WhatsApp is attractive when a reminder should let the customer ask for help in the same moment.

## Compare the obstacle resolved rather than the last click

For a hypothetical recovery review, classify replies by obstacle: delivery uncertainty, product fit or a checkout problem. Keep the email sequence and any WhatsApp follow-up on one timeline, including a buyer who completes the order before the next reminder. Define whether the goal is a useful answer, a completed checkout or incremental margin; these are different observations. WhatFlow’s inbox is useful when the reminder produces a question someone must resolve. An existing email flow can continue serving buyers who prefer it. Before expanding the sequence, remove duplicate contact and record the discount and support costs alongside attributed purchases, without presenting the dashboard’s attribution as a causal result.

Use comparable groups where practical and suppress reminders after purchase. Review overlap with existing email timing, then inspect the questions customers ask before buying.
