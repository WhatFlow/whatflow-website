## Start with the customer’s job

A checkout rule and a confirmation message solve different COD problems. A rule affects the payment or checkout experience. A message asks the customer to confirm an order or clarify a detail afterward. Treating them as the same feature leaves part of the process undefined.

## Where each approach helps

A message-only setup may be sufficient when the store’s checkout already behaves correctly. More involved COD operations may also need payment-method or fee configuration. Evaluate the two responsibilities separately and check Shopify plan or extension eligibility where relevant.

## Separate a checkout fee from a message decision

Use a hypothetical COD checkout to record three distinct observations: whether the payment option is offered, which customer-facing fee is shown and what the later confirmation asks. Have the team inspect the order total before preparing the message, then compare it with the approved wording. A checkout charge in the buyer’s order is not the software subscription, and an order confirmation is not evidence that the checkout rule behaved correctly. WhatFlow’s relevant Shopify configuration should be evaluated within the store’s supported plan and setup. Keep any other rule app in the ownership map, especially when more than one system could alter the same checkout experience.

Test checkout eligibility, the placed order and the confirmation response as separate steps. Check how a late reply affects the intended fulfillment decision.
