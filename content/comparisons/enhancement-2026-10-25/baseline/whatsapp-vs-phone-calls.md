## Start with the customer’s job

A phone call can resolve a complicated issue quickly, but it also asks the customer and employee to be available at the same moment. WhatsApp lets a shopper confirm an order or clarify a delivery detail when they have time, while leaving a readable trail for the team.

## Where each approach helps

Calls remain useful for urgent or sensitive conversations and people who prefer speaking. WhatsApp is particularly attractive for repeatable questions with a short answer. A good operation can reserve calls for exceptions rather than making every order require one.

## The practical cost difference

Include staff time and unsuccessful call attempts alongside telephony fees. For WhatsApp, include app and delivery costs plus human handling of replies. Do not claim labor savings without measuring the team’s actual process.

## A controlled test before launch

Use a controlled set of confirmation tasks. Track attempts, elapsed time, response clarity and the final order action. Compare the process cost, not just the price of a connected minute.

Write down the intended audience, trigger, message and owner before enabling the workflow. Use test recipients first and include an awkward case, not only the ideal purchase. When the buyer replies, ask another teammate to continue the conversation. The result should be understandable without relying on the first person’s memory. This is a suggested evaluation method, not a claim that we measured a particular uplift in another store.
