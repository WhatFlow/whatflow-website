## Request a destination-specific coverage and fallback record

For a hypothetical supplier evaluation, ask the RCS provider to document the intended countries, supported interaction and behavior when that route is unavailable. Require an explanation of whether fallback changes the message format, billed units or reply destination. Compare those answers with the reachable, permissioned WhatsApp audience and the exact workflow the store wants. WhatFlow supplies the Shopify application around WhatsApp; the choice still depends on customer reach and useful interaction. Review a fallback case with the support colleague before selecting the route. A successful demonstration on one device is evidence about that demonstration, not proof of coverage across the store’s entire destination mix.

Use a representative group of devices and countries rather than a single phone. Test the intended interaction, fallback and reply handling. Ensure the support team knows which channel the customer actually received.

## WhatFlow’s allowance and team costs

For a concrete baseline, consider 1,000 sent messages in a month. Among the listed WhatFlow plans, Starter produces an app-side cost of $4.99 at that volume. It is a planning example, not a combined quote for every possible message category or destination.

Keep three lines in the budget: the application, applicable WhatsApp delivery fees, and any separate implementation or services.
