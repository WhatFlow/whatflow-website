## Key takeaways

- Separate feature entitlement from message capacity.
- Price the first likely expansion before choosing a tier.
- An included feature still needs configuration and suitable data.

## Start with the customer’s job

A merchant should not have to move to a much larger software package just to use one operational feature. The frustrating moment often arrives after the initial setup, when the store discovers that a useful automation or integration belongs to another edition.

## Where each approach helps

Feature-based editions can make sense for complex enterprise products with very different buyers. For a Shopify store, the important question is whether the plan you can afford includes the workflow you need today and the one likely to come next.

Record the exact action, required connector and feature entitlement in each cell before estimating sends. ## WhatFlow’s allowance and team costs

For a concrete baseline, consider 20,000 sent messages in a month. Among the listed WhatFlow plans, Growth produces an app-side cost of $29.00 at that volume. This calculation uses its $29.00 base, 20,000 included sends and $0.0025 rate above the allowance. It assumes a single monthly allowance and does not include tax or Meta delivery charges. It is a planning example, not a combined quote for every possible message category or destination.

Keep three lines in the budget: the application, applicable WhatsApp delivery fees, and any separate implementation or services. Meta pricing varies by destination and category, and some service or qualifying utility messages have different treatment. An app allowance does not override Meta’s rules. The current installation terms and rate pages should be checked before committing a campaign budget.

Use the [WhatFlow Business API listing](https://apps.shopify.com/whatflow-official-api) to confirm the published allowance and overage basis.
