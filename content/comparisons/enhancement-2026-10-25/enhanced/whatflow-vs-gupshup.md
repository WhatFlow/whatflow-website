Evaluate Gupshup when the breadth or customization of the project justifies a platform implementation. Test WhatFlow when the required Shopify workflow is close to an existing app's scope. In both cases, compare the working system, its owner and its total cost rather than the vendor name alone.

## Feature access and the people who use the inbox

The storefront WhatsApp buttons remain free forever and can fit the Shopify theme and design. The button is an entry point, not a promise of unlimited free automated sends. Automated messaging still follows the selected allowance and applicable delivery rules. Keeping those two costs separate makes the free offering easier to understand.

## A worked WhatFlow cost example

For a concrete baseline, consider 1,000 sent messages in a month. Among the listed WhatFlow plans, Starter produces an app-side cost of $4.99 at that volume. This calculation uses its $4.99 base, 2,000 included sends and $0.005 rate above the allowance. It assumes a single monthly allowance and does not include tax or Meta delivery charges. It is a planning example, not a combined quote for every possible message category or destination.

Keep three lines in the budget: the application, applicable WhatsApp delivery fees, and any separate implementation or services. Meta pricing varies by destination and category, and some service or qualifying utility messages have different treatment. An app allowance does not override Meta’s rules. The current installation terms and rate pages should be checked before committing a campaign budget.

## Assign ownership at each system boundary

Hypothetical example: an order update depends on Shopify, an external warehouse and a conversational application. Ask the proposed Gupshup implementer to name the source event, the customer/order identifiers passed between systems and the place staff answer replies. Mark which component owns retries and corrections when the warehouse changes a shipment after the original event.

Compare only the equivalent supported scope with WhatFlow Business API, then list any remaining custom requirement separately. A broader implementation may be justified when those systems must coordinate, but the proposal should include their maintenance and operator access. The decision becomes reviewable when each boundary has a named owner rather than a platform logo standing in for the complete application.

Gupshup may be appropriate for a broader conversational infrastructure project with dedicated technical ownership and a clear implementation scope.
