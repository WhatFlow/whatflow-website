## Start with the customer’s job

A useful broadcast begins with a reason the recipient should care. A recent buyer of one product may want a related accessory, while someone waiting for stock needs a different message entirely. Sending everything to everyone wastes both attention and budget.

## Where each approach helps

A simple bulk sender can be sufficient for a genuinely uniform audience. Shopify context becomes valuable when order history, product interest or store activity should shape the communication. Permission and relevance still need to be considered for every segment.

## Explain why a recipient belongs before preparing the send

For a hypothetical accessory campaign, write one inclusion rule and the exclusions that prevent an irrelevant message. Review a few representative records: a recent buyer of the main product, someone who already owns the accessory and a customer who opted out. Check what the available Shopify data actually establishes instead of inventing product interest from a broad tag. Keep a short explanation the inbox colleague can use when someone asks about the offer. WhatFlow’s Shopify-oriented workflows can support this targeted conversation, but segmentation does not create permission. Estimate costs from the resulting eligible audience and planned messages, then review whether the replies reveal a useful product need.

Define one audience, one exclusion and one success measure. Review a sample of recipients before sending and ensure the inbox team can answer the expected product questions.
