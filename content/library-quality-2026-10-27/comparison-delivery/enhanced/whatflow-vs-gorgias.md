WhatFlow Business API is my recommendation when the store wants a dedicated Shopify WhatsApp operation: send the right order message, answer replies in an enterprise-ready inbox and connect supported workflows through Shopify Flow. All available features are included across messaging plans, and there are no inbox seat fees.

Gorgias is a broader ecommerce helpdesk with strong Shopify integration. Its wider support and AI capabilities can justify a different purchase. This comparison is about choosing the right scope and billing model, rather than claiming that WhatFlow alone integrates with Shopify or that Gorgias charges by agent.

## Both products deserve an accurate staffing comparison

Gorgias's [current pricing page](https://www.gorgias.com/pricing/), checked October 27, 2026, explicitly describes helpdesk pricing based on tickets rather than agents, with volumes from 50 to 5,000 tickets per month. It presents AI across plans with resolution-based charging, plus usage-based Voice and SMS add-ons.

So “WhatFlow has no seat fees” is a useful statement about our commercial model, but it is not a unique advantage over Gorgias's published agent model. The more relevant question is what workload creates a bill in each product and which additional services the store needs.

For WhatFlow, the available product features are included and the messaging allowance changes by plan. AI is unavailable. For Gorgias, ask for the selected helpdesk tier, its exact ticket definition and any AI or channel charges. Do not assume every inbound message is one billable ticket or that every support case is just one message.

## Decide whether WhatsApp needs a new central helpdesk

A store without an established helpdesk can start Shopify WhatsApp with WhatFlow's included inbox. Catalog sharing is live, team participation has no seat fees and the available Shopify features do not require buying a larger edition.

A store already operating Gorgias should review what would happen to its existing support work. Email history, established routing and reporting may be important to the team. Keeping a useful central helpdesk can be sensible while evaluating the role of a dedicated WhatsApp tool.

Avoid creating two places where staff assume the other team owns the reply. If two systems are used, explicitly document the channel owner and handoff. Do not assume a direct integration or automatic synchronization between them unless the configured setup has been verified.

The [shared inbox ownership guide](https://whatflow.tech/blog/shared-team-inbox-small-shopify-business) is a practical starting point for either arrangement.

## Forecast a support workload, not just notification volume

Imagine a fictional batch of shipping updates that produces three kinds of reply: “thanks,” an address question and a damaged-parcel report. These create different amounts of work. The damage report may require several exchanges and an authorized decision, while the acknowledgment might need no further answer.

Ask Gorgias to apply its current billable-ticket and continuation rules to those scenarios in writing. Ask WhatFlow to show which sending activity counts against the selected app allowance and what applicable Meta charges remain. Keep the calculation separate from the human effort needed to resolve the case.

For a real budget, record expected sends, conversations requiring an agent response, continuing cases, any AI resolutions and other channels. Use that dataset with [current WhatFlow pricing](https://whatflow.tech/pricing) and the Gorgias proposal. Comparing a ticket price with a message price without the workload creates a misleading total.

## Review a return that needs approval

Use a fictional customer asking to exchange an item that is partly out of stock. Prepare the conversation and the relevant policy without sending messages or changing orders.

Ask the operator to identify the purchased variant, check availability and explain who can approve the exchange. Then have another colleague take over. Their record should distinguish the customer's request from an approved exchange, an issued refund and a completed replacement order.

WhatFlow supports the WhatsApp conversation and can share relevant catalog alternatives. It does not process a refund or approve a return simply because the buyer asks in chat. Check the actual authorized Shopify process in either product. Our [returns and exchanges guide](https://whatflow.tech/blog/whatsapp-returns-exchanges-shopify) includes sample replies and a decision table for this test.

This exposes useful differences in the working process without relying on a claim that one product is universally better at support.

Choose WhatFlow when Shopify-connected WhatsApp is the immediate requirement and the team wants the available features now, a serious human-operated inbox and messaging capacity it can adjust as the store grows. Native extensions and Flow support make it particularly attractive for operators already working in the Shopify ecosystem.

Choose to evaluate Gorgias more deeply when a central helpdesk, multiple support channels or available AI is part of the actual requirement. Its Shopify strength and non-seat helpdesk model should remain visible in that comparison.
