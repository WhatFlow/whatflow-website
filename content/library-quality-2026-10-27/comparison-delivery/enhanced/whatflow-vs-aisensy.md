## The short answer

My starting recommendation for a Shopify WhatsApp team is WhatFlow Business API: store-focused working surfaces, full feature access across plans and no inbox seat fees. AiSensy also offers Shopify automation, so the advantage to assess is the complete daily workflow and commercial model, not exclusive integration. Use the actual regional AiSensy package only where it offers a better fit for a defined need.

I would choose WhatFlow Business API when a Shopify team wants WhatsApp to feel like part of running the store: order-linked messages, native extensions, Shopify Flow and an inbox for the replies. Every available feature is included across plans, and the inbox has no seat fees.

AiSensy is worth evaluating for its broader WhatsApp platform and the package offered in your market. Its free offering, automation add-ons and fair-use terms need to be compared as published, rather than replaced with a blanket claim that it charges every team by the seat.

## Compare the actual regional package

AiSensy's [pricing page](https://aisensy.com/pricing/), checked October 27, 2026, presents Free Forever and paid offerings alongside separate chatbot and AI add-ons. The Indian pricing includes five drag-and-drop chatbots at ₹2,500 per month, or ₹2,250 per month under annual billing. Check the selected country, currency and billing term before relying on a card's amount.

Its “unlimited” plan also has published fair-use caps, including 200 agents, 500 tags, 100 attributes and 50 segments. Those are specific terms, not proof that a small store will reach them. They belong in the evaluation when the proposed design depends on the relevant resource.

Keep country messaging rates separate from the software package. Do not infer a Meta policy change solely from a provider's pricing announcement; check the applicable [WhatsApp platform pricing](https://business.whatsapp.com/products/platform-pricing) directly for the message category and destination.

## Decide whether a chatbot is part of the job

A customer asking about an order may need an accurate reply and a colleague who owns the case. That requirement does not automatically call for a new chatbot project. Start with the questions the store receives and the decisions a human must make.

WhatFlow's enterprise-ready inbox currently excludes AI. Its Shopify Flow integration can support workflows built from available triggers, conditions and actions; that is different from claiming an AI agent can decide every response. [Flow with connected Shopify apps](https://whatflow.tech/blog/shopify-flow-whatsapp-app-integrations) is useful when the rule and source event are clear.

If an AI or chatbot experience is a launch requirement, examine AiSensy's exact proposed add-on, its maintenance and the human handoff. Include those costs rather than comparing the bot-enabled proposal with a bare inbox subscription. Where a human team and deterministic store workflow solve the problem, WhatFlow's included features make a strong starting point.

## Inspect the variant behind the message

Build a nonsending example with a product that has two similarly named variants. Prepare a sample customer question, the proposed outgoing content and the data fields supplying it. Then change the image or availability of one variant.

Ask the operator to identify:

1. The source of the product title and image.
2. Which variant is actually being recommended.
3. Whether the displayed information requires a new sync or another update.
4. Where the customer's reply will be reviewed.

Repeat with a missing image and a multi-item order. A working field substitution is useful, but it does not prove that every product combination produces a clear customer message.

WhatFlow's live catalog sharing helps the agent show the relevant item in the conversation. Its [catalog-support guide](https://whatflow.tech/blog/shopify-whatsapp-catalog-support) explains the distinction between a managed catalog, an externally managed one and the final purchase step. Check AiSensy's proposed store-data route against the same scenario; do not assume it lacks the capability without reviewing it.

## Budget for the workflow you will operate

Give the providers a single brief with the monthly sending forecast, destination mix, support roster, required automation and optional chatbot or AI scope. Record the billing unit for each item and what happens beyond its allowance.

WhatFlow's [messaging plans](https://whatflow.tech/pricing) vary by allowances and rates, while all available features remain included from Free upward. Inbox participants create no seat charge. Applicable Meta fees are separate. That structure is particularly helpful when a small team needs the full workflow now and wants to increase sending later.

For AiSensy, retain the free and unlimited-access terms that actually apply, then add the required package and add-ons. Comparing a limited launch against a fully expanded chatbot project would distort the result in either direction.

The [app-cost audit](https://whatflow.tech/blog/audit-shopify-app-costs-feature-gates-seat-fees) provides a place to record currency, billing period, tax and implementation assumptions alongside the recurring total.

## The Shopify decision

Favor WhatFlow when the customer question begins with a Shopify order or product and the team wants the message, reply and next store action to stay closely connected. Native extensions, Flow support and a no-seat-fee inbox are concrete reasons to choose it.

Keep AiSensy on the shortlist when its regional package or verified wider WhatsApp tools meet a requirement the store has identified. Let the changed-variant exercise and the complete proposal decide that fit. For the Shopify-focused workflow, start with WhatFlow's included tools and expand sending capacity as the store needs it.

## Questions worth asking

### Is WhatFlow a practical AiSensy alternative for Shopify?

WhatFlow’s advantage is the combination of Shopify-native working surfaces, complete feature access and an inbox that does not introduce a per-person subscription as the team expands. AiSensy can work for teams that prioritize its wider WhatsApp campaign ecosystem and the available local commercial package.

### How should I compare AiSensy pricing with WhatFlow?

AiSensy separates the WhatsApp platform subscription and message credits from optional chatbot and AI add-ons. Check the selected region, billing period, included agents and actual add-ons rather than comparing a single headline price.

### Does WhatFlow charge per inbox seat?

No. WhatFlow’s team inbox has no seat fees. Adding colleagues does not create a per-user subscription; the messaging plan and actual usage still apply.

### Are WhatFlow features restricted on the free plan?

No. All plans have the same feature access. Message allowances and rates differ, and applicable Meta delivery charges are separate. Theme-matched WhatsApp buttons are free forever.
