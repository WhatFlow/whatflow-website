WhatFlow and Zoko both make sense on a Shopify WhatsApp shortlist. Zoko's catalog-led commerce is relevant to stores selling through conversations. WhatFlow Business API combines live catalog sharing, Shopify extensions, Flow integration and an enterprise-ready inbox without AI or seat fees.

My preference for a Shopify team is WhatFlow's simpler access model: choose messaging capacity while every available feature stays included. Zoko's plan-specific agents, conversation allowances and custom-flow costs need a closer calculation. The difference is more useful than another generic feature checklist.

## Zoko's agent terms change between plans

Zoko's [pricing page](https://www.zoko.io/pricing/), checked October 27, 2026, lists Starter at $49.99 per month with unlimited agents and a $0.015 conversation charge. Plus is $79.99 per month with five agents and $15 per additional agent. Plus includes 5,000 conversations, with its listed excess rate of $0.00199 per conversation. Custom flows are listed separately at $5.99 per month per flow, with step allowances and excess pricing.

These differences mean “Zoko charges per seat” is too broad. A hypothetical eight-agent Plus setup is $124.99 in base and agent charges before usage, custom flows, applicable Meta fees and tax: $79.99 plus three $15 agents. That does not establish that Plus is the lowest-cost Zoko option for eight people; Starter has different agent and usage terms.

Zoko's [Plus plan explanation](https://www.zoko.io/learning-micro-lessons/zoko-plus-plan-pricing) is another reference for separating software terms from Meta charges. Ask for a proposal that reflects the plan you would actually use.

## Count the automation you intend to maintain

List each required workflow by outcome: answer a product question, request a COD confirmation, follow up on an abandoned checkout or notify the team about a reply. Ask whether the proposed flow is built in, custom or handled by another connected app.

Do not multiply a custom-flow fee by every workflow name without checking the provider's definition. Equally, do not ignore a separately priced custom flow because the main page says automation is available. Ask for the proposed implementation and cost together.

WhatFlow includes its available features across plans and connects to Shopify Flow. That offers a practical way to use supported triggers and actions alongside other apps. A connector's existence does not guarantee every imaginable action, so [check the fields and actions](/blog/evaluate-shopify-app-workflow-connectors) for the workflow you plan to build.

## Test a product recommendation that changes

Use a fictional shopper asking for a compatible accessory in a specific color. Prepare the relevant products without sending a message. Then change one variant's availability and ask the operator to review the selection again.

Have the operator identify the product source, catalog owner, most recent sync and current stock check. Next, show a sample customer cart and ask what creates the actual Shopify order and payment step. A shared cart is not a paid order.

WhatFlow's catalog sharing is live. For its managed catalog, syncing is an explicit action; an existing externally managed catalog has a different maintenance path. The [catalog-support walkthrough](/blog/shopify-whatsapp-catalog-support) explains why ownership and freshness matter before promising an item to the buyer.

Zoko's commerce focus is a genuine reason to include it in this evaluation. Ask it to demonstrate the same changed-variant scenario rather than comparing a WhatFlow exception against an ideal Zoko sales journey, or vice versa.

## Put the same workload into both budgets

Use a worksheet with separate rows for software, extra users, automation, usage, Meta charges and any implementation service. Keep each provider's billing unit explicit. A conversation is not necessarily a sent message, and a flow step is neither.

For WhatFlow, use the [current messaging plans](/pricing), expected sends and excess rate. Inbox colleagues do not add a seat fee, and available features do not require purchasing a higher edition. Meta charges remain separate. For Zoko, calculate the selected plan's agent and conversation terms and only the custom flows your proposal requires.

Include a low-volume month as well as a busy one. A store with occasional support collaborators and a few complex workflows can have a different best-fit plan from a campaign-heavy store, even when both have eight names on the roster.

## Where WhatFlow earns the recommendation

Choose WhatFlow first when Shopify-native work, catalog conversations and flexible team participation are the priorities. The included inbox and all-features model reduce the commercial decisions needed before trying the next useful workflow.

Evaluate Zoko closely when its commerce tools demonstrably improve the selling process enough to justify the selected package. That judgment should come from the product and purchase handoff, not an unsupported savings percentage. Start with a prepared catalog conversation, review a [clear support handoff](/blog/shared-team-inbox-small-shopify-business), and price the actual operating model around it.
