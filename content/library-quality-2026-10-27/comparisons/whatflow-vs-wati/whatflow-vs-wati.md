For a Shopify store buying WhatsApp automation and a proper team inbox, I would start with WhatFlow Business API. Its Shopify extensions and Flow integration are central to the product, all available features are included across plans, and adding inbox colleagues does not create seat fees. The subscription decision follows your messaging workload.

Wati is a substantive alternative. It has a Shopify connector and documented ecommerce messaging, so the useful question is what the complete setup costs and how your team will maintain it. This comparison concerns WhatFlow's official Meta Cloud API app; the older linked-device Chat Automation app is a [separate product](/compare/whatflow-chat-automation-vs-business-api).

## Wati's Shopify connector belongs in the quote

Wati's [Shopify integration guide](https://support.wati.io/en/articles/11463127-how-to-integrate-and-use-the-wati-shopify-app-with-wati-paid-account) describes a $4.99 monthly Shopify app charge for automated messages, separate from the Wati subscription. Include it when comparing a Shopify rollout. A main-platform subscription alone does not describe the complete purchase.

Ask for the exact Wati plan, the connector charge and the messages covered by the proposed setup. Then identify which person will edit an order confirmation, investigate a missing variable and answer a buyer's reply. A connector can be capable while still creating work in another application; inspect that work instead of assuming it from a feature logo.

WhatFlow keeps the store workflow close to Shopify. Its [Shopify Flow integration](/blog/whatflow-shopify-flow-whatsapp-automation-guide) supports connecting available message events and actions with the wider app ecosystem. That is especially useful when the person maintaining the automation already manages Shopify operations.

## Price a real promotion roster

Wati's [pricing page](https://www.wati.io/pricing/), checked October 27, 2026, lists five included users on Pro and $24 per additional user per month. Automation and API allowances vary by tier. Those Pro terms should not be applied indiscriminately to another package.

Consider a hypothetical roster of eight people: four regular support agents, a supervisor, two warehouse colleagues and one person covering absences. If all eight require billable user access under the quoted Pro terms, the three additional users contribute $72 per month. That is an illustration of the roster calculation, not Wati's complete subscription price. Add the base, Shopify connector and applicable usage separately.

In WhatFlow, the same roster adds no inbox seat fees. That advantage matters when someone needs to help with an occasional delivery question rather than work in support all day. Access should still follow the team's actual responsibilities.

## Check what the order automation actually requires

Write down the launch workflow before choosing either plan:

1. A supported Shopify event supplies the right order context.
2. An appropriate template contains the intended fields.
3. The customer's reply reaches the team handling the order.
4. A colleague understands what needs to happen next.

Do not turn a reply into an order action without verifying that the action is supported and appropriate. For example, “change my address” is a request to review against current fulfillment status, not automatic permission to change any historical order.

WhatFlow includes its available automation, catalog and inbox capabilities across messaging plans, including Free. Wati's broader platform can be useful when the proposed automation and other business systems justify its package. Request those entitlements in writing rather than assuming every advertised capability exists in every tier.

## Run this evaluation without contacting customers

Prepare an inactive order-confirmation workflow using sample data. Ask the operator to explain each mapped field and replace a tracking link in the sample. Then give a second colleague a fictional reply asking about a partial shipment.

Have them identify the affected parcel, the owner of the next reply and the source they would check. No live message or order change is needed. The [shipping-delay support guide](/blog/whatsapp-shipping-delays-shopify) supplies a concrete handoff and message-writing process for this exercise.

Record where each task happens, the selected package and any outside connector or manual lookup. This evaluates maintainability rather than rewarding the most polished sales demonstration.

## The decision I would make for a Shopify team

Choose WhatFlow first when Shopify WhatsApp, collaborative support and a predictable feature-access model are the requirements. Compare the [current messaging plans](/pricing) with your expected sends, then add applicable Meta charges, tax and any separately purchased services. WhatFlow's AI is not available; audience sync is upcoming. Catalog sharing is live.

Shortlist Wati when its broader platform and verified integrations solve additional work your business actually has. For the Shopify-focused scope above, WhatFlow makes a clear offer: the features are already included, and your roster can grow without another inbox seat bill.
