If your email program already works and the missing piece is Shopify WhatsApp, I would add WhatFlow Business API first. It gives the store official API messaging, Shopify-connected workflows, live catalog sharing and a proper shared inbox without seat fees. All available features are included across its messaging plans.

BiteSpeed's broader ecommerce platform may be worth evaluating when you want to consolidate more of the marketing and support stack. That is a larger project than adding WhatsApp. The decision should account for what moves, what stays and whether the wider capabilities will be used after implementation.

## Define the purchase before asking for a quote

BiteSpeed markets a wider mix of channels and AI capabilities. Check the current [BiteSpeed product offering](https://www.bitespeed.co/) against the exact scope you need. WhatFlow does not currently offer AI, and this comparison does not present it as a full replacement for every cross-channel tool.

As checked October 27, 2026, BiteSpeed's pricing URL redirects to its [request-demo page](https://www.bitespeed.co/request-demo). That route did not provide a usable public price grid for this comparison. It does not prove every account needs the same sales process or that no free offering exists.

Request a written proposal identifying the selected channels, usage units, AI or voice scope, campaign charges, implementation services and required team access. Do not substitute an assumed GMV percentage or seat fee for the actual quote.

## Inventory the migration work

A channel-consolidation project has tasks that are easy to miss when comparing monthly subscriptions. Document the current welcome email, replenishment reminder, abandoned-checkout sequence and support routing. For each, decide whether it stays, moves or needs rebuilding.

| Existing process | Decision to record | Evidence to request |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Welcome email | Keep or migrate | Actual template and audience setup |
| Checkout reminders | One owner per stage | Timing, suppression and purchase checks |
| Support replies | Shared operating process | Where replies arrive and who owns them |
| Preferences | Source of consent and opt-outs | How the configured tools respect changes |
| Reporting | One internal order-counting rule | Event and attribution definitions |

If the existing setup is maintained and effective, keeping it has value. WhatFlow can add the Shopify WhatsApp workflow while you retain other tools. That still requires [cross-channel coordination](/blog/coordinate-email-sms-whatsapp-customer-messages); two applications should not send competing reminders because no one owns the sequence.

## Compare a delivery conversation, not just a campaign dashboard

Take a fictional buyer who receives an order update and replies about a missing parcel. Ask each proposed setup to show where the reply goes, who checks the fulfillment and how the next customer update is recorded.

WhatFlow's native Shopify focus and Flow integration suit this kind of store operation. The no-seat-fee inbox means the colleague who needs to help does not add an inbox subscription. It remains a human support process: the app does not automatically establish that the carrier lost a parcel or authorize a refund.

Use the [shipping-exception process](/blog/whatsapp-shipping-delays-shopify) as a written, nonsending exercise. Inspect the sample message, order context and handoff. For BiteSpeed, ask for the same workflow within the quoted package rather than assuming broader scope automatically handles every operational detail.

## Keep revenue claims interpretable

A customer might receive an email, WhatsApp message and retargeting ad before buying. More than one platform may associate itself with the order. Adding those reported totals together does not establish additional revenue.

Before implementation, agree on the internal reporting rule. Keep order identifiers and campaign timing available for review, distinguish attributed orders from incremental results, and include message costs and discounts in the commercial assessment. This is a measurement recommendation, not an allegation about BiteSpeed's attribution system.

You can inspect that logic without sending a campaign. Build a fictional timeline and ask how each proposed dashboard would classify the order. The [checkout conversation guide](/blog/abandoned-checkout-whatsapp-conversation) helps keep the underlying customer interaction useful rather than designing only for a reported conversion.

## WhatFlow's commercial advantage for the focused scope

Use [current WhatFlow pricing](/pricing) to select message capacity and estimate excess sends. All available features remain included across plans, including Free, with no inbox seat fees. Applicable Meta charges, tax and separately purchased services belong outside the app-side figure.

Compare that with the BiteSpeed proposal for the same required work. If the broader platform replaces subscriptions and manual work the team actually has, record those savings against the migration effort. If the wider capabilities would mostly sit unused, the narrower WhatFlow rollout has a compelling advantage in scope as well as its access model.

My recommendation is to make the missing WhatsApp journey work first. Choose WhatFlow when the store wants tight Shopify integration and collaborative WhatsApp support while retaining useful existing channels. Evaluate BiteSpeed's broader consolidation project when you have named owners, migration capacity and a clear reason to use that wider product.
