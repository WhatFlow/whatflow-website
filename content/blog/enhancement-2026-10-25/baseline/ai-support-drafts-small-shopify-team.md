AI can help a small team turn a rough explanation into a clearer reply. It can also produce a confident answer to a question the store has not actually resolved. The useful boundary is between assistance with wording and authority to decide what happened to a customer’s order.

Choose a narrow first task, such as shortening a verified policy explanation or drafting an internal outline. Keep the source information and the review step visible. A fluent paragraph should not be treated as evidence that a refund, shipment or product capability exists.

## Start from verified information
Give the drafting process a current, approved explanation of the relevant policy or product detail. Avoid asking it to invent the answer from a vague customer question. If the team does not know whether an accessory fits, the correct next step is to check, not to request more persuasive wording.

Keep changing facts separate from reusable language. Delivery status, inventory and account actions need current system information. A saved prompt or an old conversation cannot establish the present state of a particular order.

## Limit the information shared with the tool
Use the minimum customer context needed for the task and follow the store’s approved tool and data-handling process. Many writing exercises can use a generalized question without names, addresses or a complete private conversation.

Do not paste credentials, private attachment links or unrelated customer records into a drafting system merely because it accepts a large prompt. A useful assistant should reduce writing effort without creating an unnecessary second copy of sensitive operational information.

## Make the reviewer’s task concrete
Check the factual answer, promised next step, tone and any limitation the buyer needs to understand. Verify that the text does not claim an action has already happened when it is only planned. Review links and product versions rather than assuming they remain correct.

Read the answer against the original question. An AI draft may summarize the general topic while missing the buyer’s specific concern. The final reply should resolve the practical uncertainty or clearly explain what will be investigated next.

## Use natural language to describe workflow intent carefully
Tools such as Shopify Sidekick can assist with supported workflow drafting, but the generated steps still require review. The event, conditions, recipient and actual action matter more than whether the diagram looks plausible. Keep examples inactive while checking the configuration.

If a needed WhatFlow Flow task is missing from the draft, select the supported installed task manually rather than assuming another action is equivalent. Standard visual configuration can avoid API code, but it cannot remove the need to understand which record the workflow will affect.

## Keep the human conversation available
A customer reporting a difficult issue may need a person to interpret context and make an appropriate decision. Do not disguise every automated message as a personal staff response. Be honest about the process and the team’s response expectations.

WhatFlow’s team inbox can support the human handoff, with no inbox seat fees on Business API plans. The relevant message format and channel requirements still apply. AI-generated wording does not automatically become an approved WhatsApp template or permission to contact a new audience.

## Evaluate time saved and errors avoided
Review a sample of assisted replies for accuracy and unnecessary follow-up. Faster drafting is useful only if the team does not spend the saved time correcting invented promises. Keep examples of recurring mistakes and adjust the approved source material or the task scope.

The goal is a clearer, dependable answer produced with less repetitive writing. Keep judgment, current system facts and consequential decisions connected to the appropriate human or supported process. That makes AI assistance useful without asking the customer to absorb its uncertainty.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can an AI draft prove that a refund or shipment happened?

No. Verify the current system state before the reply claims an action is complete.

No. The approved-template process and channel requirements apply independently of how the wording was drafted.
